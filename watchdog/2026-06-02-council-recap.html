Council Adds a Fiscal Check,
Then Hits Pause on New Investment.

A Watchdog statement from the Broomfield County Republicans on the June 2, 2026 City Council meeting.

FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE · Broomfield, Colorado · June 3, 2026

At its regular meeting on Tuesday, June 2, the Broomfield City Council moved a 20-year Xcel Energy franchise toward the November ballot, paused new data center projects, added a fiscal review step for major land use decisions, and updated the city’s contracted trash service. The Broomfield County Republicans give Council credit where it earned it, and we flag the votes that deserve a closer look from taxpayers.

What follows describes what Council actually did, item by item, followed by our response. Every action below passed on the night of the meeting. Where we quote a council member, the words are taken from the official meeting video and are linked to the exact moment so any neighbor can listen for themselves.

A franchise agreement and a fee, headed to your ballot

On first reading, Council advanced Ordinance No. 2307, a new 20-year franchise agreement with Xcel Energy for the use of city rights of way, and referred the question to voters at the November 3, 2026 election. The current agreement expires at the end of 2027. Under a franchise like this one, Xcel collects a franchise fee from Broomfield customers on their monthly bills and remits it to the city. The vote to advance was unanimous.

We are glad this one goes to the voters rather than being settled at the dais. A franchise fee is a cost that lands on every household and business utility bill, and a 20-year term locks in those terms for two decades. Before November, residents deserve plain numbers: what the franchise fee adds to a typical monthly bill, how much revenue the city expects to collect, and what the city gives up or keeps under the new terms. We will be watching for that transparency and we encourage every voter to read the ballot question closely.

A moratorium that tells investment to wait

Council gave first-reading approval to Ordinance No. 2313, a temporary moratorium on new data center facilities. Staff explained the pause would last up to 18 months, or until the city writes regulations, and that no data center is currently proposed in Broomfield. The vote was unanimous.

Writing clear rules before a rush of applications is reasonable. Our concern is the signal a moratorium sends. Data centers are among the larger contributors to a commercial tax base, and Broomfield’s own finance staff have called the budget fragile. A pause that stretches toward a year and a half, with the door to investment closed in the meantime, risks sending good jobs and new tax revenue to neighboring communities while Broomfield deliberates.

The discussion also showed where some members want this to go. Council Member Paloma Delgadillo (Ward 2), who is up for election in 2027 and campaigns as a champion of affordable housing and higher-density development, pressed for using zoning and review hurdles to control the use:

“maybe make it a use by special review and kind of put those hurdles up so we can have a really robust review process.”

Council Member Paloma Delgadillo, June 2, 2026, first reading of the data center moratorium (video, 3:47)

Council Member Laurie Anderson (Ward 4), also up for election in 2027 and a member of the Colorado Communities for Climate Action coalition, supported acting now, before any project is on the table:

“this is the right time to do it because nobody’s here yet.”

Council Member Laurie Anderson, June 2, 2026, first reading of the data center moratorium (video, 3:34)

We read those comments as a preference for more process and more hurdles, and they fit the record. On housing and growth, Council Member Delgadillo has been a steady advocate for higher density and has declined to oppose state bills that override local limits on dense development. The BCRC view is that Broomfield should be writing rules that say how to welcome investment, not rules that signal the city is closed for business. Property owners and employers should know Broomfield wants them here.

Credit where it is due: a fiscal check on land use

Council also gave first-reading approval to Ordinance No. 2306, which adds fiscal impact review criteria to the standards for rezonings and Planned Unit Development plans. In plain terms, before approving major land use changes, the city will weigh the long-term cost and revenue effects on the budget. After amendments, the vote was unanimous.

This is the kind of discipline the Broomfield County Republicans have asked for. Putting a real fiscal test in front of big land use decisions protects taxpayers and helps the city grow its base instead of its obligations. We commend Council for adopting it and we will watch to see that the review is used honestly and not treated as a box to check.

City trash service, updated

On a second reading and public hearing, Council passed Ordinance No. 2310, updating the rules for the city’s contracted residential waste collection service. The vote was unanimous.

Trash, recycling, and compost service is something every household pays for, so the details matter. As these changes take effect, residents deserve a clear answer on what they will pay and what choices they keep. We will follow the rollout and report back if costs climb.

Also on the agenda

On the consent agenda, Council adopted Resolution No. 2026-81, signing Broomfield on to updated policy positions of the Colorado Communities for Climate Action coalition. Two members who are up for election in 2027, Mayor Pro Tem Jean Lim and Council Member Laurie Anderson, sit on that coalition, so the city is adopting positions shaped by a group its own members help steer. Council also advanced Ordinance No. 2309 on first reading, a ballot question asking voters in November to change the mayor’s term from two years to four years, with a two-term limit. Both passed. We will keep an eye on what the climate coalition’s agenda asks of Broomfield’s budget, and we will have more to say on the mayor’s term question as it heads toward the ballot.

A statement from the Chair

“Tuesday night was a split decision for taxpayers,” said Heather Stidham, Chair of the Broomfield County Republicans. “Council did something right by attaching a fiscal test to big land use decisions, and we thank them for it. But pausing new investment for up to a year and a half, while the city calls its own budget fragile, is the wrong message to send. Every member of this council ran and governs as a progressive Democrat, and it shows. There is no one at the dais whose first instinct is to protect taxpayers and welcome investment. Welcome the jobs and the tax base, give voters honest numbers on the Xcel franchise fee, and tell residents what each of these decisions will cost before the bill arrives.”

How residents can stay involved

Council meetings are held most Tuesdays at 6 p.m. at One DesCombes Drive, and meetings are streamed online. Agendas and recordings are posted at the city’s agenda portal. The Xcel franchise and the mayor’s term question are both expected on the November 3, 2026 ballot, so this is the season to pay attention. We encourage every Broomfield neighbor to watch, show up, and weigh in.
