Broomfield’s Comp Plan Wish List
Must Pass the Fiscal Reality Test.

A Watchdog statement from the Broomfield County Republicans on the Broomfield Together comprehensive plan update.

FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE · Broomfield, Colorado · June 1, 2026

Broomfield has opened public input on “Broomfield Together,” the once-a-decade rewrite of the city’s Comprehensive Plan and Transportation Mobility Plan. The Broomfield County Republicans are urging residents to weigh in now, and to insist that every idea in the plan be measured against what the city can actually afford.

The Comprehensive Plan guides budgets, zoning, and city policy for the next 10 to 20 years. The priorities set this summer will shape our neighborhoods long after the meetings end. Early committee input is leaning toward higher-density housing, taxpayer-subsidized housing, and greater reliance on RTD transit. Every one of those carries real and recurring costs, and they arrive at a time when the city’s own finance staff have downgraded the 2026 budget to “fragile,” citing a roughly 6 million dollar drop in property tax revenue and soft sales tax.

Where we stand

Broomfield’s appeal is its neighborhoods and open space, not stacked, urban-style density. We support property owners’ rights and orderly growth, and we oppose blanket upzoning and density mandates that change the character of established neighborhoods.

We support a healthy housing market, and we oppose government acting as a housing developer. The right tool is removing regulatory barriers and fees so the private market can build, not taxpayer-subsidized units that raise costs for everyone else.

We favor maintaining and improving the roads residents actually use over committing local dollars to expanded RTD service and rail stops that few will ride. With the budget rated fragile, the city should not plan around an agency whose fares cover only a small share of its operating cost.

Above all, we ask that the city attach a real cost to every proposal before it becomes a goal. Plans should grow the tax base by filling vacant commercial sites and attracting employers, and should maintain what we already own before building new obligations we will pay to operate forever.

A statement from the Chair

“This plan is being written as if money is abundant, while the city’s own finance team calls the budget fragile,” said Heather Stidham, Chair of the Broomfield County Republicans. “We are not against planning. We are for honesty about the bill. Protect our neighborhoods, grow the tax base, maintain what we own, and tell taxpayers what each idea will cost before it becomes policy.”

How residents can weigh in

Public comment is open now. Residents can make their voices heard in two easy ways:

  • Take the survey at BroomfieldVoice.com/BroomfieldTogether
  • Attend a community open house (the same content is offered at each):
    • Tuesday, June 17, 6 to 8 p.m. · Aloft Broomfield, 8300 Arista Pl
    • Tuesday, June 23, 6 to 8 p.m. · Aspen Lodge at Anthem Ranch, 16151 Lowell Blvd
    • Wednesday, June 24, 6 to 8 p.m. · Broomfield Community Center, Lakeshore Ballroom, 280 Spader Way

Local decisions like these are where residents make the biggest difference. We encourage every Broomfield neighbor to take part.
