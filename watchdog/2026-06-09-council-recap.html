Council Approves More Density,
Then Studies a Carbon Filter for City Decisions.

A Watchdog statement from the Broomfield County Republicans on the June 9, 2026 City Council meeting.

FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE · Broomfield, Colorado · June 10, 2026

At its regular meeting on Tuesday, June 9, the Broomfield City Council heard a pitch for Front Range passenger rail, approved a new residential development at Cobblestone Crossing that required a Comprehensive Plan amendment, and gave staff direction to start building greenhouse gas and environmental review into how the city makes decisions. The Broomfield County Republicans give Council credit where it earned it, and we flag the items that deserve a closer look from taxpayers.

What follows describes what Council actually did, item by item, followed by our response. Where we quote a council member, the words are taken from the official meeting video and its captions, and we point to the moment in the meeting so any neighbor can listen for themselves. Captions are auto-generated and can include small errors, so we encourage residents to confirm against the recording.

A passenger rail pitch, paid for in part by your RTD taxes

Under reports, representatives of the Joint Service Rail Partnership and Front Range Passenger Rail briefed Council on plans to move toward commuter and intercity rail along the Front Range, including the long-promised Northwest Rail corridor that runs through Broomfield. Presenters told Council the regional boards have adopted a term sheet and begun appropriating the first dollars for design and preliminary engineering, and that the RTD board voted 14 to 1 in late April to spend roughly 5.58 million dollars from its FasTracks Internal Savings Account, a fund that Broomfield residents pay into through the RTD sales tax, to advance station design and engineering.

Broomfield families have already paid the RTD sales tax for years on the promise of Northwest Rail, and they have every right to ask when, or whether, they will ever see the train. We are wary of pouring more local transit dollars into a project whose ridership and fare revenue remain in question while the roads residents drive every day still need maintenance. Before Broomfield commits any further money or staff time, taxpayers deserve plain numbers: the total cost to finish, the share Broomfield ratepayers will carry, the projected ridership, and how much of the operating cost fares are expected to cover. A presentation is not a commitment, and we will be watching closely if this moves toward one.

More density at Cobblestone Crossing, and a Comprehensive Plan amendment to allow it

On a public hearing, Council approved Resolution No. 2026-69 for the Lambertson Farms (Cobblestone Crossing) residential application. The approval covered a Planned Unit Development plan amendment, a final plat, a site development plan, and a Comprehensive Plan amendment to fit the added housing, a project that drew residents to the podium with concerns about density and traffic safety near a school and a playground. Council Member Paloma Delgadillo (Ward 2), who is up for election in 2027, moved to approve, and Council Member Heidi Henkel (Ward 5), also up for election in 2027, seconded. The motion passed 7 to 0.

We support private property rights and a healthy housing market, and we would rather see market-priced homes built by the private sector than taxpayer-subsidized units. Council members noted that these are for-sale homes rather than subsidized housing, and that point is fair. But two things give us pause. First, the city amended its own Comprehensive Plan to allow the added density, the same long-range plan residents are being asked to help rewrite this summer, and changing the plan to fit a project is the opposite of letting the plan guide the project. Second, the traffic and safety concerns that neighbors raised were real, and the approval leaned on conditions to sort them out later. We will be watching to see that the traffic engineering conditions are honored and that the safety worries near the school are addressed in fact, not just on paper.

A greenhouse gas filter for city decisions

Under requests for future action, Council took up Council Member Sean McKenzie’s request to build greenhouse gas emissions and environmental information into the city’s decision making. After discussion, Council reached a consensus to direct staff to begin the work, starting with the Advisory Committee on Environmental Sustainability and folding it into the 2027 budget and the Comprehensive Plan conversation. Council Member McKenzie (Ward 4) is not up for election until 2029. He described the goal as embedding an environmental review step throughout city operations:

“build that filter through which we will begin to say, what are the GHG implications”

Council Member Sean McKenzie, June 9, 2026, request for future action on greenhouse gas and environmental information (official meeting video, approx. 4:16:20); he called the change “a paradigm shift.”

To her credit, Council Member Henkel, who is up for election in 2027, voiced the same worry we have. She cautioned against turning an environmental goal into a reason to deny projects or to pile new requirements on employers:

“I just don’t want this to be a reason for denial. I do worry about businesses. We’re kind of shutting up businesses out of Colorado in general.”

Council Member Heidi Henkel, June 9, 2026, discussion of the greenhouse gas request (official meeting video, approx. 4:16:45); she later said the city is “just kind of overregulated” on similar mandates.

Our view is straightforward. A new layer of greenhouse gas review, applied to budgets, land use, and contracts, is a standing cost in staff time and a fresh hurdle for the businesses and builders Broomfield needs. The city’s own finance staff have called the budget fragile. This is exactly the season to be careful about adding process that grows government and slows investment. We thank Council Member Henkel for raising the business-climate concern out loud, and we urge the full Council to keep that caution front and center as staff bring this back. If the review ever becomes a reason to say no to good projects, taxpayers and job seekers will pay for it.

Also on the agenda

Staff gave a water drought update and introduced a proposed change that would move commercial and industrial customers from a flat water rate to a tiered rate structure. No rate was adopted that night, but a tiered structure can mean higher water bills for some businesses, so we will follow it as it develops. On the consent agenda, Council approved professional services agreements tied to two road projects, the Midway Boulevard Multimodal Improvements (Resolution No. 2026-78, with Wilson and Company) and the West 120th Avenue Complete Streets project (Resolution No. 2026-77, a third amendment with Muller Engineering). Road work residents use is a core city job, and we simply ask that the city keep these contracts on budget and focused on the lanes and intersections people actually drive.

A statement from the Chair

“Tuesday night told you a lot about the direction of this council,” said Heather Stidham, Chair of the Broomfield County Republicans. “They amended the Comprehensive Plan to allow more density, they leaned in on rail that Broomfield families have paid for and still cannot ride, and they set out to build a carbon filter into city decisions while the budget is called fragile. We give credit where it is due. These were for-sale homes, not subsidized units, and Council Member Henkel was right to warn about chasing businesses out of Colorado. But the pattern is more process, more mandates, and more cost. Protect our neighborhoods, respect property owners, welcome employers, and tell taxpayers what each of these ideas will cost before it becomes policy.”

How residents can stay involved

Council meetings are held most Tuesdays at 6 p.m. at One DesCombes Drive, and meetings are streamed online. Agendas, packets, and recordings are posted at the city’s agenda portal. The Comprehensive Plan rewrite is underway this summer, and the greenhouse gas review will come back to Council after staff work, so this is the time to pay attention. We encourage every Broomfield neighbor to watch, show up, and weigh in.
