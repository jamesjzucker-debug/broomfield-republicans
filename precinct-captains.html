Be The Republican
On Your Block.

Broomfield has roughly 50 precincts. We want one trained, active Republican captain in every single one of them. About 4 hours a week in election season. Full training, full support, and a team of neighbors around you.

Why this matters

Suburban elections are won by neighbors talking to neighbors.

A paid canvasser knocks 30 doors and forgets the conversation. A precinct captain knows their neighbors by name, knows which house has a new puppy, and is trusted by 50-100 households who would never open the door for a stranger.

That trust is the asset that wins close races in Broomfield. It's also why we organize at the precinct level — not just from the top down. Republicans in this county will not out-spend or out-advertise the Democrats. We will out-organize them at the doorstep.

This is the role that actually wins elections in suburbs like ours.

High impact

Time and term, on the level.

Average: 4 hours per week during election year, 1-2 hours per week off-cycle.

Spikes: 8-10 hours/week in October of an election year, plus about 6 hours total for the March precinct caucus.

Term: two years, aligned with the caucus cycle. Renewable.

You won't be alone — every captain has a Ward Captain mentor above them and a small support network.

The job, honestly

What a precinct captain actually does.

Six things. We don't hide the ball.

1. Know your precinct.

Maintain an up-to-date list of every registered Republican and persuadable unaffiliated voter in your 250-400 household precinct. We provide the data and the tools.

2. Knock the doors.

Hit every R and U household at least twice in an election year. Once to introduce yourself; once at GOTV. We provide the walk lists and the scripts.

3. Recruit the block.

Over time, identify and recruit a Block Captain for every 20-30 houses. You're not just a doorknocker; you're a leader of a small team.

4. Run the caucus.

Organize attendance at the March precinct caucus. Make sure our delegates get elected and our voices get heard at the county and state assemblies.

5. Report briefly.

One-paragraph weekly check-in to your Ward Captain during sprint months — doors knocked, problems, requests for help. 5 minutes a week.

6. Show up.

Monthly captain dinner (free food, real food). Quarterly all-hands training. Annual recognition dinner with spouses invited. The community is part of the job.

Full support

You are not improvising. We've built a system.

Saturday boot camp

Six-hour Saturday training. Six modules. Lunch provided. By the end of the day you'll know your precinct's voter list, you'll have practiced the door scripts, and you'll have your first walk scheduled.

Ward Captain mentor

Every precinct captain reports to a Ward Captain who manages 8-10 precincts in their geographic area. They walk your first precinct with you and check in weekly during election season.

Voter data and lists

You get access to the GOP Data Center — the RNC's voter file. We provide walk lists, scripts, doorknockers, and the technology to log conversations.

Monthly captain dinner

First Tuesday of every month. Free dinner. Half social, half a single training topic. You'll know every other captain by first name.

Recognition and community

Captain of the Month. Annual Recognition Dinner (spouses invited). Captain hat, lapel pin, name tag. You're part of something built to last.

A path that goes further

The best precinct captains often become Ward Captains, future candidates, or executive committee members. This isn't a dead-end role — it's the starting line.

Honest about the role

What a precinct captain is NOT.

Not a fundraiser.

We will not burn you out by asking you to sell gala tickets. Asks for money go through the Chair, not through captains.

Not a social-media warrior.

We actually ask captains to keep their captain identity off public political posting. Your neighbors trust you because they know you locally, not because of your X account.

Not a candidate (yet).

Some captains do later run for office. But the captain role is not about you running — it's about organizing your block for the candidates we collectively support.

Not alone.

Every captain has a Ward Captain mentor above them and a goal of 3 Block Captains beneath them within their second year. You're part of a small team, not a solo act.

Step one

Apply to be a Precinct Captain.

Three minutes. We'll get back to you within a week and schedule a one-on-one with the Chair, Vice Chair, or the Ward Captain for your area.

Not sure yet? Email the Chair with questions, or come to a captain dinner as a guest before deciding.
